### What must a Q/K normalization, RoPE and cache-write fusion preserve?

**Card:** `attention-qk-rope-cache-fusion` · **Type:** `performance_candidate` · **Evidence:** `source_observed`

**Axes:** `cache_layout`, `numerical_contract`, `data_movement`

#### Applicability

- `operator_families`: <code>qk_norm_rope_cache</code>
- `target_languages`: <code>flydsl</code>, <code>hip</code>, <code>gluon</code>

#### Use when

- Q/K preparation produces data consumed by attention or an indexer cache.

#### Try

- Keep normalization weights/epsilon, rotary pair convention, positional indices and cache slot mapping identical to the unfused path.
- For the wave64 builder, enforce its even rotary dimension and vector divisibility requirements before attempting packed FP8 stores.
- Separate model-lifetime tensor adapters from request-lifetime tensors: the source caches static weights/cos/sin, while request tensors must not be retained as static adapters.

#### Rationale

- The implementation couples norm/RoPE with vectorized stores and contains separate wave32/wave64 builders and static-adapter lifetime handling.

#### Keep as alternatives

- Keep a sequence of norm, RoPE and cache write operations as the same-contract baseline.

### How should a skinny split-K decode GEMM that feeds a fused epilogue be tiled and ordered so the weight is read from HBM only once?

**Card:** `splitk-decode-gemm-single-weight-pass-and-xcd-remap` · **Type:** `performance_candidate` · **Evidence:** `reported_experience`

**Axes:** `data_movement`, `execution_geometry`, `cache_layout`, `numerical_contract`

#### Applicability

- `operator_families`: <code>qk_norm_rope_cache</code>, <code>gemm_epilogue_fused</code>
- `target_languages`: <code>triton</code>
- `gfx`: <code>gfx950</code>

#### Use when

- Decode projection with few tokens (M up to 32 in the recorded step) and a large weight, so weight streaming dominates the GEMM
- Split-K partials go to an fp32 workspace and are reduced by a separate fused epilogue (norm, RoPE, cache write)
- Multi-XCD gfx (gfx950, 8 XCDs) where program ids are dispatched round-robin across XCDs
- The summation order is fixed by the split-K slice count and ascending-K tl.dot accumulation inside each slice

#### Try

- Choose BM >= M so a single M block covers every row and each weight tile is loaded once; the recorded step used BM 16 for M<=16 and BM 32 for M>16
- Launch a 1-D grid of N/BN*SK programs and remap pid so each XCD owns a contiguous 1/8 of the (N tile, K slice) space, with K slice fastest
- Load the weight with the .cg cache modifier and keep the activation load masked on M
- Retune BN, num_warps and num_stages per M bucket, but keep the split-K count and K-slice width fixed so the fp32 partials stay bitwise identical
- When an installed library launcher does not expose BM or the grid order, copy its kernel locally and launch it directly, keeping its accumulation order

#### Rationale

- With BM < M every M block re-reads the whole weight; the engineer's GEMM microbench went from 27.5 to 18.5us when BM=32 covered M>16
- The engineer chose the per-XCD contiguous order with K slice fastest as the best of the pid orders tried and reported +5-10% on the GEMM at M<=16 (comparison baseline not stated)
- Per the engineer, .ca and no modifier were 2x slower than .cg on the weight stream, and .cs matched .cg
- Because split-K plus ascending-K fixes the fp32 order, the engineer found every swept BM, BN, BK, warps and stages config bitwise identical to the installed partials

#### Keep as alternatives

- Keep the library split-K partial kernel with BM smaller than M, which streams the weight once per M block
- Other pid orders: K slice fastest globally, per-XCD chunk with N tile fastest, identity order, or one K slice per XCD
- Cache modifiers .cs, .ca or none on the weight load
- Tuning waves_per_eu instead of tile geometry

#### Limits

- Over-padding M did not pay at M<=16: the engineer's microbench had BM=32 slower than BM=16 there (20.7 vs 17.3us with BN32)
- The copied kernel has no M loop and requires M <= BM; larger (prefill-like) M needs a fallback path
- The remap assumes 8 XCDs with round-robin dispatch and that N/BN*SK divides evenly by 8
- The engineer judged M<=16 to be near the streaming ceiling: a plain .cg streaming read of the same 75.5MB was slower than the GEMM
- At M=24/32 the engineer still measured about 2-3us more than at M=8, and attributed it to the fp32 partials traffic (4.7MB at M=32), which this change does not address
- In the engineer's sweep, waves_per_eu 1 and 2 hurt and 4 and 8 were neutral
- Evidence is one recorded step on a bf16 gfx950 Triton decode projection
